Hand motion estimation in the wild via IMUs and gaze-tracking: Can an efficient linear model suffice?

E. Gerber, N. E. Krausz

One of the major current challenges in robotics is the simulation-to-reality (sim2real) gap for training robot controllers particularly due to the limited ability of simulations to recreate real physics. This limitation could be partially mitigated by training controllers on human recordings. Transhumeral prostheses, meant to accurately recreate the motion of the missing part of the limb, could also benefit from such controllers. In this work, we propose a controller for transhumeral prostheses that utilizes a network of sensors worn by the user to enable naturalistic and intuitive control of the prosthesis, and demonstrate proof-of-concept hand motion predictors trained on an open-access dataset, based on a) linear regression and b) a UNet. The training data included 8 subjects, and we tested using a 9th subject. Our results demonstrate that the upper arm position is an important input for hand motion prediction, and adding additional sensor modalities can add benefit. Both methods generalized well to the test subject, yet surprisingly, the UNet’s prediction error was only ∼24% lower than the efficient linear model’s while requiring substantially more computation. Future work will extend this towards a sensor network to help address dynamics and hand kinematics during grasping and manipulation for a proposed transhumeral prosthesis controller.

Preliminary Results on Deep Sensor Fusion for Endpoint Prediction in Transhumeral Prostheses

E. Gerber, N. E. Krausz

This study presents a multi-modal sensor fusion framework for endpoint prediction in transhumeral prostheses. By combining gaze tracking, IMUs, and sEMG data evaluated on pilot experimental data and the open-access Project Nymeria dataset (8 subjects, 16 hours of data), linear regression and modified UNet predictors achieved 3D hand endpoint MAEs of 17.3 cm and 11.8 cm respectively, demonstrating strong generalizability across subjects and tasks.

Deep Learning Based Sensor Fusion for Endpoint Prediction and Control of Transhumeral Prostheses

E. Gerber, N. E. Krausz

This research introduces a sensor fusion framework using a hybrid CNN-GRU deep learning model to predict endpoint coordinates for above-elbow prostheses. By combining gaze tracking, IMUs, and sEMG, the controller aims to enable simultaneous and naturalistic control. Using pilot data, the model was trained to predict 3D coordinates of the hand and wrist, achieving an average mean absolute error of approximately 65 mm on unseen test trials.

Preliminary Results of Gaze Tracking and Hand Motor Synergy Coordination During Reach-to-Grasp VR Tasks for Improved Prosthetic Arm Intent Prediction

E. Gerber, N. Krausz

This research investigates the spatiotemporal coordination between gaze and hand motion to address the high cognitive burden of current sequential prosthetic control. By transforming VR reaching data into a specialized "eye-hand" reference frame, we identified that gaze peak acceleration consistently leads hand peak acceleration by approximately 150 ms. While this temporal synergy suggests that user intent can be inferred early in the motion, we are moving toward a multi-modal approach—integrating sEMG, kinematics, and gaze—to develop a more robust, naturalistic prosthesis controller.

Gaze and Motion Synergies in VR Reaching Tasks towards Multi-Joint Prosthetic Arm Control

E. Gerber, N. Krausz

This research investigates gaze and motion synergies during reaching tasks to improve the control of multi-joint prosthetic arms. By analyzing kinematic data in egocentric coordinates, we found that gaze velocity typically anticipates hand velocity and that their positions converge prior to a grasp. Current efforts involve developing a multi-modal platform to record sEMG, gaze, and kinematic data during real-world activities to leverage early motor synergies for user intent prediction.
