About me

The person behind the research

I'm an M.Sc. student in Mechanical Engineering (Robotics & Control) at the Technion, working in Dr. Nili Krausz's eNaBLe Lab. My research asks how much of a person's intended movement can be predicted from the body's own signals (gaze, arm motion and muscle activity), with the goal of naturalistic, intuitive control of above-elbow prosthetic arms.

In my IEEE BSN 2026 paper, I predicted hand position from upper-arm motion and gaze using about 18 hours of in-the-wild recordings. A causal UNet-GRU reached about 14 cm error on an unseen subject, yet a simple linear model came within roughly 24% of it at a tiny fraction of the compute, a trade-off that shapes how I choose models. I'm now developing a multi-modal fusion model that adds high-density sEMG, trained on a dataset we're collecting with optical motion capture, eye tracking and EMG.

Outside the lab, I enjoy photography, particularly wildlife, and I believe that careful observation, whether through a camera lens or a sensor array, is the foundation of good science.
